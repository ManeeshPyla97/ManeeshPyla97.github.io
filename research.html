Research

Low-energy electron attachment, electronic resonances, and how solvation changes them.

Overview

When a low-energy electron attaches to a molecule, it can form a temporary anion known as an electronic resonance. These short-lived states can decay by ejecting the electron or lead to bond breaking, which is one route by which electrons cause damage to molecules, including biomolecules. My work characterizes these resonances: their energies, widths (lifetimes) and decay channels.

Dissertation focus: characterization of electronic resonances using single and multireference wave-function methods.

Orbital stabilization techniques applied to pyridine to locate and characterize electronic resonances, and a follow-up study of partial widths of shape resonances in pyridine and uracil.

Papers: J. Chem. Phys. 161, 154306 (2024); J. Chem. Phys. 164, 014308 (2026)

How microsolvation and solvation shift and reshape the low-lying π resonances of uracil, a nucleobase relevant to radiation damage of nucleic acids.

Papers: Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 27, 3588–3601 (2025); J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 17, 4561–4568 (2026)
